History of Hawaii

Pre-Christian Hawaii

Somewhere between 940 and 1200AD, Polynesian peoples arrived on the islands of Hawaii. Ancient Hawaii was run as a kingdom, with some aspects being similar to the caste-system of India and was recognized as a world power by other countries. Many of the families with power were believed to have descended from certain gods and goddesses.

Hawaii, like many other places, has deeply rooted spiritual history. Ancient Hawaiian religion was polytheistic, meaning they believed in multiple gods and goddesses (na akua). The four main Gods are Kane god of creation, Ku god of war, Lono god of peace and fertility, and Kanaloa god of the ocean. Hawaiian legends say Pele (ka nahine `ai honura) is the creator of the islands. She is the goddess of fire, lighting, and volcanos. She is claimed to live in hale maumau crater, and active volcano on one of the islands, and people often leave her offerings when visiting volcanos or even during potential eruptions. Another notable figure, one popularized by more recent western media, is Maui the demi-god. He was a protector of Hawaii and is said to have balances the day and night of the seasons.

Pre-colonial Hawaii notably had moe aikane which was same sex relationships. These relationships were common among chiefs and there was little stigma attached to sex. It was also common to practice polyamory. Many people, also very common among chiefs, had multiple wives or husbands at one time.
