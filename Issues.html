Why is this happening?

Economic Gap in Hawaii

The main issue that has led to the displacement of so many native Hawaiians is the gap between average wages and the living costs. People move from the mainland often because they are finacially well off, and have stable employment and savings. While Hawaiians struggle to find good jobs, and often are born into lower-income families. This leads, for many, no choice but to move to the states where there are better chances for employment. This gap is continuing to widen over time.

Developers Buying up land

Lana'i, the sixth largest of the Hawaiian Islands, is 98% owned by one billionare names Larry Ellison. 65% of Farmland in Hawaii is foreignly owned, and 11% of land is owned by only 37 billionares. Much of this land is put to use as a place for resorts and hotels. While so many native Hawaiians struggle to find housing and jobs, developers and billionares hold onto valuable land for personal profit.

U.S. Control

The U.S. holds claim to mnay territories such as Puerto Rico, Guam, and Hawaii. The U.S. has control over Hawaii's land and resources, often at the expense of native Hawaiian communities. U.S. territories cannot vote in the U.S. elections and are only represented in senate with no voting power. Poverty rates are higher in these places, and are more prone to natural disasters geographically. Various policies and practices put in place without Hawaiian voices negativley effect the community, including the forced relocation of native Hawaiians from their ancestral lands, the establishment of military bases, and the exploitation of natural resources for economic gain. The ongoing struggle for sovereignty and self-determination continues to be a central issue in Hawaii's political landscape.
